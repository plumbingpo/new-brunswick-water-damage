Typical Water Damage Problems in Elizabeth

Starting at the top of a home and working toward the ground is a handy way to think about water damage. That is the route we take when inspecting a property in Elizabeth, and five spots along it account for most calls.

Attics: Rain that slips past a damaged shingle or a loose vent boot lands on the attic floor first. Because insulation absorbs so much, a leak overhead may run through several storms before a mark appears below. Once soaked, that material no longer does its job and holds moisture against the ceiling joists. Our part is removing what is saturated and drying the wood, while a roofing contractor closes the opening.

Bathrooms: Bathroom floods usually come from a forgotten bathtub, a stopped-up sink or a toilet that will not quit rising. Even a tiled floor lets water pass at the grout, the tub edge and the base of the walls. In an upstairs bathroom the next stop is the ceiling of the room underneath. Anything that came out of the toilet bowl should be treated as dirty water and cleaned with disinfectant.

Walls: Plumbing hidden in a wall may seep at a joint for weeks without leaving a puddle anywhere. The hints are small: a baseboard that swells, a stain that grows, or a patch of wall that feels cool and soft. On these calls in Elizabeth, an infrared camera and a pin meter show us how far the dampness reaches. After the pipe is repaired by a plumber, we open just the damp part and dry the cavity.

Tanks: The hot water tank sits out of sight in a garage, closet or utility space and stays full all the time. Drips from the relief valve, the drain valve or the fittings on top are early signs that parts are wearing. If the tank lets go, shut the cold inlet valve over the heater and turn off its power or gas supply. Our New Brunswick Restoration crew extracts the water and checks the nearby walls and flooring, which often get wet from behind.

Drains: The lowest drain in the house is where a sewer blockage shows itself. If murky water comes up in a downstairs shower or floor drain, waste has nowhere to go but back inside. It falls under Category 3, meaning heavily contaminated, so cleanup calls for proper protective gear. Stop using water in the house and keep people and pets away until a trained crew arrives in Elizabeth.

How Elizabeth Homeowners Can Avoid Water Damage

A home usually gives a few hints before a leak turns serious. Our technicians see the same early clues again and again in Elizabeth, and they are worth learning. Blistering paint, ceiling stains, warped baseboards and floorboards that curl at the edges each point to water behind or beneath them. Add a stale odor in an enclosed space, a loose toilet, or a faucet that keeps dripping.

None of these is an emergency on its own, but each is cheaper to fix now than later. Re-caulk tubs and showers when the bead cracks, since water behind tile does damage you will not see for months. Test a sump pump several times a year by filling the pit until the float trips and the motor runs. Keep stored items off the basement or garage floor on shelves or in plastic bins.

No amount of maintenance prevents every leak. What it does is make them rarer and smaller, and that matters when the alternative is days of drying equipment in your Elizabeth living room.

How Drying Works on an Elizabeth Water Damage Job

Homeowners in Elizabeth often want to know what living with drying equipment is actually like. On day one, the crew extracts the water, removes anything that cannot be dried, and sets air movers and dehumidifiers in the affected rooms. Expect noise on the level of a few big fans, plus noticeable warmth in the room. It needs to stay on around the clock, and turning it off, shifting it or airing out the room slows everything down.

Each day after that, a technician visits to take moisture readings in the same spots and record them. As areas dry, we move machines to the places that are lagging and take away the ones no longer needed. The technician shares the readings and explains the trend.

Drying usually lasts 3 to 5 days. It can run longer when water sat for a while before it was found, when it reached insulation or hardwood, or when several floors are involved. Once all points test dry, New Brunswick Restoration collects the machines and hands over the moisture log for your Elizabeth home. Only then should rebuilding start, because closing up damp walls traps the problem inside.
